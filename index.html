02 Three Pilots For pepelwerk

Three Shows, One Format, One Pilot To Start

Three versions of the idea we talked through on the call: real people, a warm sit-down with Ari asking the questions, a two-day shoot. Each one could become a returning series. They cost the same to make, so the only real question is which story you tell first. We shoot that one as a pilot, with a trailer, before you commit to a season, and the per-episode price comes down as the season grows.

01
Pilot 01 · The flagship · could become a series
"Future-Proof"
Estimated cost per episode: $45k (12 episodes) - $55k (6 episodes), based on a 2-day shoot.
A woman settles into a chair under studio lights and starts telling the story of the year her job disappeared. The voice asking the questions is warm and curious, and it belongs to no one in the room. The interviewer is Ari.

Future-Proof is a documentary interview show, one real person per episode. Each guest is someone whose role was automated away, got laid off, or whose degree never turned into work, and who now has a real job to tell it from. Ari, pepelwerk's AI assistant, draws the story out from off camera, curious and firmly on the guest's side, and the show says plainly that pepelwerk is how the turnaround happened. You made the comparison yourself on our call: Ari can be for pepelwerk what the gecko is for Geico, a brand character people recognize on sight. This show is where that character gets built.

The audience already voted for this topic. Choosing a Future-Proof Career earned 39,446 views on your channel, more than thirteen times your next best video, and you never put a dollar of promotion behind it. This show is built for the community you named on the call, the roughly 8 million Americans trending toward unemployment.

You meet the guest on the worst Monday of her working life. You hear Ari ask the question a polite human interviewer might skip, and you're there when she says her new job title out loud. Hot Ones (the interview show where celebrities answer questions while eating progressively hotter wings) proved that one odd twist in a familiar interview format gets people past their rehearsed answers and into something true. An AI in the interviewer's chair is that twist, and every episode hands the audience the same payoff: proof that somebody who was exactly where they are found a way out.
02
Pilot 02 · Reinvention · could become a series
"Second Act"
Estimated cost per episode: $45k (12 episodes) - $55k (6 episodes), based on a 2-day shoot.
The plant where he worked for twenty-six years is closed, and the trade he spent those years mastering no longer shows up in job listings. He is fifty-four, and retirement is more than a decade away.

Second Act points the same interview chair at reinvention. Each episode is one person starting over in the back half of a working life, after the industry shrank or the role stopped existing. Ari asks the questions, warm and unhurried, and follows the guest into the new work: the classroom, the certification, the first shift in a different uniform. pepelwerk sits in the story as the place the new goal job came through.

On our call you said the story worth telling is the outcomes, not the software. Your own Purpose and Progress page is already counting those outcomes in public: 403 work goals and 1,229 learn goals set so far. Each of those goals belongs to a person who could be sitting in this chair.

The first half of the episode is what ended, and the second half is what he built next. By the time the cameras find him on the new job, you are rooting for a man you met twenty minutes ago. Queer Eye (the Netflix series where five experts spend a week rebuilding one person's confidence along with his wardrobe and his house) runs on exactly that feeling, and audiences return to it season after season. Second Act raises the stakes, because the thing being rebuilt is a livelihood. For the viewer living the same story, it is an hour with someone who already made it through.
03
Pilot 03 · First jobs · could become a series
"First Shot"
Estimated cost per episode: $45k (12 episodes) - $55k (6 episodes), based on a 2-day shoot.
She did everything the plan said to do. The degree is on the wall, the applications went out for months, and the callbacks never came. So the story she tells from the studio chair starts with the quiet phone, and Ari wants to hear all of it.

First Shot covers the front end of a working life, one recent graduate per episode, each story running from the months of silence to a first real job. Ari walks her back through where the search actually turned: the skills she verified in her Talent Wallet, and one of the 368 self-paced courses already on the platform. That is what put her in front of an employer hiring for the roles your homepage says are being designed right now.

Watching it, you keep score with her: the first callback, the interview that runs long, the offer read out loud on camera, and then the first day at the job.

Last Chance U (the Netflix series that follows junior college football players fighting for one more shot at a roster spot) made millions of people root hard for young athletes the system had already written off. First Shot borrows that rooting interest for graduates the job market keeps overlooking. It gives the viewer with the same degree and the same quiet phone someone her age who actually landed, and shows exactly how she did it.
03 Lead With Value

The Legendeering Framework

The methodology behind every Talex production, and behind the three concepts above. Five directives, one core principle.

Legendeering is Talex's proprietary strategy. The core principle is straightforward: lead with value, always. The brand earns trust by giving without expectation, and that trust grows over time because people keep choosing to watch.

Five directives drive it:

  • Give Without Expectation. Value delivered without strings. The kind of generosity that builds loyalty money cannot buy.
  • Be Relentlessly Authentic. Say true things. Show real work. Speak like a human being.
  • It's Not About You. It's About Them. The audience has to come first. It's all about their wants and needs and making something they will choose to watch.
  • Start With Story, Follow With Facts. Story gives information shape, context, and emotional weight. Facts alone rarely move people.
  • Consistency Is Key. Trust is built in moments, repeated over time.

pepelwerk knows how to get a person from where they are to the work they actually want. We'd make one as a pilot to start, and all three concepts came straight out of the method above.
